This is a small standalone Apple Watch app for ear training: a note plays and the user names it. There is no iPhone app, no account, no network access, no microphone use, and no third-party code.

To answer your questions directly:

1. Demo: A video is attached, filmed on an Apple Watch Series 10 running the latest watchOS. Apple Watch has no built-in screen recording, so it is filmed with an iPhone. It shows the complete app: launch, a round (a note plays, the user picks one of four names, sees the result, taps Next), the Settings screen including the My instrument mapping, and the Stats screen. That is the entire app. There are no accounts and no user-generated content.

2. Purpose and audience: A practice tool for music learners who want to recognize notes by ear, from beginners learning Do Re Mi to players of instruments that name notes differently from concert pitch (for example the saz, where the string called Re commonly sounds E). It solves the problem that ear training needs many short repetitions with immediate correction, and a watch is always on the wrist for a quick round. The My instrument setting lets the app use the learner's own note names.

3. Setup and access: None required. Launch the app and tap Start. A note plays right away; tap one of the four names to answer. Replay is unlimited before answering. Next plays a new note. Settings offers Do Re Mi or A B C names, Standard or My instrument naming, and seven notes or notes with sharps and flats. Stats shows today's and all-time results. There are no logins, credentials, or sample files.

4. External services: None. The app has no network access at all. Tones are synthesized on the watch. No data providers, authentication, payments, analytics, or AI services.

5. Regional differences: None. The app functions identically in every region. The interface is localized into English, Japanese, and Turkish; in Japanese the solfège names are shown in katakana.

6. Regulated content: Not applicable. The app contains no protected third-party material and requires no authorizations or licenses.

Tested on: Apple Watch Series 10 (watchOS 26) on the wrist, plus Apple Watch SE 3 40mm and Series 11 46mm simulators. Privacy: no data is collected or transmitted; settings and practice counts are stored only on the watch.

I hope this clarifies everything. Please let me know if you need anything else.
